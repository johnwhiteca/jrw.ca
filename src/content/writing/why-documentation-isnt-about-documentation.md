Documentation is often viewed as a necessary administrative task. Something to complete after the real work has been finished.

I have never seen it that way.

Good documentation is not created to satisfy a process. It exists to reduce uncertainty for the next person.

Sometimes that next person is a colleague.

Sometimes it is your future self.

Context disappears faster than we think

Every project begins with conversations, assumptions, and small decisions that seem obvious at the time.

Weeks later, many of those details have already faded.

Documentation preserves more than facts. It preserves context.

It explains not only what was done, but why it was done.

Documentation is an act of service

The best documentation is written with someone else in mind.

It anticipates questions.

It reduces frustration.

It shortens the distance between uncertainty and understanding.

That is why documentation is not really about documentation.

It is about helping people move forward with confidence.
